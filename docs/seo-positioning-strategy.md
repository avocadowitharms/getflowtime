# Flowtime positioning and SEO strategy

## Architecture audit

Flowtime is a static site. The homepage and indexes are hand-authored HTML. Guides and comparison pages are Markdown files under `content/guides` and `content/comparison`, rendered by `scripts/render-content-pages.js` into `guides/<slug>/index.html` and `comparison/<slug>/index.html`. The sitemap is generated by `scripts/generate-sitemap.js` from those Markdown files plus the homepage, guide index, comparison index, and legal/support pages.

Canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata, Twitter metadata, and Article/BlogPosting JSON-LD are generated for Markdown content. The homepage has SoftwareApplication, WebSite, Organization, and FAQ structured data. Robots allows normal indexing and points to `sitemap.xml`. App Store and Google Play links exist in the hero and download panel. Plausible is installed; no Search Console performance export is present in the repo.

## Page intent classification

Group A, core identity pages: homepage, FAQ, download panel, major feature sections. These can lead with ADHD, time blindness, hyperfocus, external time cues, reminders, and attention hijacking while preserving focus timer, time tracker, Pomodoro, and app blocker language.

Group B, problem-focused educational pages: time blindness, hyperfocus, phone distraction, gentle reminders, four-hours-later, focus timer to stop focusing. These can use ADHD strongly where the search intent calls for it, with careful non-medical wording.

Group C, generic category pages: simple focus timer, flexible focus timer, app blocker, focus timer vs time tracker, work/study/deep-focus content, existing workflow and privacy/time-tracking guides. These should remain broad and mention ADHD only as one use case.

Group D, comparison pages: existing Flowtime vs Toggl/Forest/Session/Focus To-Do/Be Focused, best app for tracking time, ADHD focus timer vs Pomodoro, best focus apps for ADHD, focus timer vs time tracker/Pomodoro. These should answer the comparison query first and use ADHD differentiation only when it clarifies a real distinction.

Group E, personal founder stories: I Use a Focus Timer to Stop Focusing, Why I Removed Account Creation From My Time Tracker. These can use first person and Flowtime origin context without implying universal experience.

## Existing overlap findings

`time-tracking-for-neurodivergent-minds` already covers neurodivergent time tracking broadly, but it does not duplicate the requested focused articles on ADHD time blindness, hyperfocus exits, phone distraction, or gentle reminders.

`sometimes-the-problem-isnt-getting-into-flow-its-getting-out-of-it` overlaps with hyperfocus and stopping, but it is a broader product-design article with an established URL. The new hyperfocus and founder articles target more specific search intent and link naturally to the older piece.

`how-long-should-a-focus-session-be` overlaps with flexible focus timers. The new flexible timer article is broader/deep-work focused and links back to the session-length guide instead of replacing it.

Existing competitor comparisons were preserved because they serve generic/commercial search intent.

## Topic clusters and batches

Batch 1, core differentiation: Why ADHD Makes You Lose Track of Time; ADHD Hyperfocus: How to Stop Without Killing Your Focus; I Use a Focus Timer to Stop Focusing; Why Pomodoro Doesn't Always Work for ADHD.

Batch 2, high-intent problem content: Why Do I Forget Why I Unlocked My Phone; Gentle Time Reminders for ADHD Time Blindness; How I Reduce Phone Distractions When My ADHD Brain Wants to Check Everything; Do You Start Working and Suddenly It's Four Hours Later.

Batch 3, broader SEO expansion: Focus Timer, Time Tracker, or Pomodoro App: Which One Do You Need; How to Use a Focus Timer Without Interrupting Your Best Work; What Is an App Blocker, and When Is It Actually Useful; A Simple Focus Timer for Work, Study, and Deep Focus.

Batch 4, comparison and discovery: ADHD Focus Timer vs Pomodoro: What's the Difference; The Best Focus Apps for ADHD Aren't Always Planners; existing competitor comparison pages.

## Internal linking strategy

Time-blindness content links to gentle reminders, hyperfocus, and flexible focus timers. Hyperfocus content links to the founder story, Pomodoro/ADHD, and flexible timer content. Phone-distraction content links to app blocking and the personal phone-distraction article. Generic timer content links to work/study use cases, app blocking, and comparison pages where relevant. Comparison content links back to educational guides only when it supports the decision.

## Medical and claim review notes

All ADHD claims should remain cautious: use phrases such as "many people with ADHD describe," "can," "may," "for some people," and "in my experience." Flowtime should not be described as diagnosing, treating, curing, or managing ADHD. Claims about time blindness, hyperfocus, and executive function are written as lived-experience/product language and should be reviewed by a human before publication if stronger scientific claims are added later.

## Measurement plan

Technical indexing check: 1 to 2 weeks after deployment. Early Search Console impressions and query review: 4 to 8 weeks. Meaningful content performance review: 8 to 12 weeks. Topic-cluster review: 3 to 6 months. Track indexed pages, impressions, clicks, queries, average position, outbound store clicks, and article-assisted store visits where available.
