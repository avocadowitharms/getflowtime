Flowtime vs Focus To-Do

Compare Flowtime and Focus To-Do for Pomodoro sessions, task lists, project tracking, reminders, and low-friction personal productivity.

01

Summary

Focus To-Do combines task management with Pomodoro timers. Flowtime does not try to manage your whole task list. It focuses on flexible sessions, projects, reminders, local history, and simple reports.

02

Quick comparison

| Criteria | Flowtime | Focus To-Do |

| --- | --- | --- |

| Primary focus | Time tracking and focus sessions | Task lists with Pomodoro timers |

| Best for | People with an existing task system | People who want tasks and timers together |

| Timer style | Open-ended Flow sessions or intervals | Classic Pomodoro structure |

| Task management | Minimal | Detailed lists, subtasks, reminders |

| Setup | Start quickly | More organization first |

| Reports | Session and project history | Task and Pomodoro statistics |

03

What Focus To-Do does well

Focus To-Do is a good fit for people who live in task lists. It combines planning and execution: create tasks, break them into subtasks, schedule reminders, and complete them through Pomodoro intervals.

If you like the Getting Things Done style of organizing, or if you want one app for lists and timers, that combination can be helpful.

It is especially useful when the main problem is deciding what to do next and then working through a defined checklist.

04

What Flowtime does differently

Flowtime does not require tasks at all. You can simply start a session and track what you are working on. Projects are available when you want context, but they are not a heavy planning system.

That matters if you already use a notebook, calendar, Obsidian, Notion, Things, Todoist, or another task manager. You may not want your timer to duplicate that work.

Flowtime is more about awareness: how long did the session last, what project did it belong to, and what patterns show up over time?

05

Choose Focus To-Do if

Choose Focus To-Do if you want to manage tasks, subtasks, recurring work, reminders, and Pomodoro sessions in the same place.

It may be the better choice if your productivity depends on detailed checklists and fixed intervals.

06

Choose Flowtime if

Choose Flowtime if you want flexible focus sessions, do not want to maintain another task list, prefer local-first tracking, or want a timer that starts with less setup.

It may be the better choice if productivity for you starts with pressing start, not reorganizing a list.

07

Common mistakes when comparing them

One mistake is assuming every timer needs task management. Some people need tasks in the same app. Others find that it makes the timer feel heavier.

Another mistake is using a task app as a time tracker and then never reviewing time. Tasks and time are related, but they answer different questions.

08

A practical decision test

Open your current task system and ask whether it already helps you choose what to do next. If it does, adding another task list inside your timer may create duplicate maintenance. In that case, Flowtime can sit beside your existing setup and track the time you spend doing the work.

If your current system does not help you choose, Focus To-Do may be more useful. A combined task and timer app can make the next action clearer, especially if you like breaking work into small checklist items.

Also notice whether you want to review tasks completed or time spent. A task list tells you what got checked off. A time tracker tells you where attention went, including the work that was necessary but not neatly captured as a completed task.

09

What to watch after choosing

After a week, look at the kind of maintenance the app creates. Focus To-Do should make it easier to plan and complete tasks, not create a second list that needs constant grooming. Flowtime should make it easier to track sessions, not leave you wishing every session had a task attached.

If you keep duplicating information between apps, simplify. Either move planning into the task app or keep planning elsewhere and let the timer stay focused on time.

FAQ

Does Flowtime include to-do lists?

No. Flowtime focuses on timing, projects, reminders, history, and reports rather than task management.

Is Focus To-Do better for Pomodoro?

Focus To-Do is a strong choice if you want a Pomodoro timer tightly connected to task lists.

Can Flowtime handle open-ended sessions?

Yes. Flowtime supports open-ended Flow sessions as well as more structured intervals.

Which app is simpler?

Flowtime is simpler if you only need tracking and focus support. Focus To-Do is more complete if you want tasks and timers together.

If your task list already lives somewhere else, Flowtime gives you a lightweight way to track the time you spend actually doing the work.
